---
name: Idle downtime reports
description: Owner-reported timing pattern for production interruptions, with limits on what it proves.
---

The owner reports that they have not noticed downtime while actively using the app; the interruptions they have noticed occur while the app is idle or unused. On 2026-11-02, they also reported that most growing timeout/network-failure notices appear around phone wake. Their screenshots showed an 8.6-second authentication wait and a 1,480,032 ms network wait, both marked as backgrounded. Later that day, they reported that waking a second app/client while one remains foreground can be followed by similar symptoms on the foreground client after the waking client settles. They also reported that a warm return after backgrounding can remain unhealthy, while fully closing and reopening has worked. Screen recordings show cold launches reaching the app's server-baseline/snapshot UI and Pack views with pending-sync indicators; the owner separately identified one recording as a stopped-auto-tracking case and another as a button tap with visible feedback but no confirmed action. These are user-reported timing and recovery clues plus client-side evidence, not proof that a background job caused the interruption, one client corrupted another, a cold reopen fixed the underlying cause, or the server spent the browser-measured time processing.

**Why:** This makes background work, pool acquisition, timers, readiness during sparse traffic, wake-time client recovery, volatile client state, and cross-client interaction important correlation targets. A cold reopen clearing symptoms may reset client state but can also coincide with network or server recovery. Browser wait can include time suspended in the background; aggregate probes still cannot establish user-request impact or causality.

**How to apply:** In production investigations, compare warm return with cold reopen and correlate both with background-worker, database-pool, request, and client responsiveness telemetry. Include a two-client case where one stays foreground while another wakes. Keep user observation separate from independently measured evidence; distinguish client elapsed wait from server processing, and do not infer the initiating cause from timing alone.
