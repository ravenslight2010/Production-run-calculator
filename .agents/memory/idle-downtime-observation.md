description: Owner-reported timing pattern for production interruptions, with limits on what it proves.
---

The owner reports that they have not noticed downtime while actively using the app; the interruptions they have noticed occur while the app is idle or unused. On 2026-11-02, they also reported that most growing timeout/network-failure notices appear around phone wake. Their screenshots showed an 8.6-second authentication wait and a 1,480,032 ms network wait, both marked as backgrounded. Later that day, they reported that waking a second app/client while one remains foreground can be followed by similar symptoms on the foreground client after the waking client settles. They also reported that a warm return after backgrounding can remain unhealthy, while fully closing and reopening has worked. Screen recordings show cold launches reaching the app's server-baseline/snapshot UI and Pack views with pending-sync indicators; the owner separately identified one recording as a stopped-auto-tracking case and another as a button tap with visible feedback but no confirmed action. They later narrowed the touch symptom: switching tabs still works, but controls inside tab screens can stop responding; timers may continue while auto-tracking stops, sometimes after a manual edit followed by Resume or a peer wake. On 2026-11-05, the owner reported that during a particularly busy workday the app was opened only once and no production run was started. This is a useful low-use observation, not server telemetry. These are user-reported timing and recovery clues plus client-side evidence, not proof that a background job caused the interruption, one client corrupted another, a cold reopen fixed the underlying cause, or the server spent the browser-measured time processing.

On 2026-11-06, the owner reported a Stop request banner remained for more than five minutes until refresh. The screenshot still showed the run as active while Stop was pending. Additional screenshots showed a cancelled `/sync/today` read with a 1 ms browser wait and a separate transport failure with a 276 ms wait; the browser reported online but sync-blocked. These are browser-side outcomes: cancellation does not establish whether the server accepted the request, and the short transport wait does not establish server processing time. Refresh returning the UI to normal does not prove that the queued Stop was applied.

Also on 2026-11-06, the owner said wake/recovery problems are more common during an active run, while one between-run wake appeared normal. A screenshot pair showed a successful no-run-to-active-run start transition, with a provisional Start state followed by Pause/Stop controls and a fresh production snapshot. That confirms the start worked, not that an already-active run survives background/wake. The owner later reported the Stop-requested banner still present after returning to the app. On 2026-11-09, two recordings compared the app before and after force-close/reopen while between runs. The before clip showed an active run and “Syncing automatic count”; sampled after-reopen frames still showed that status, although the Stop-requested state had cleared. The app also changed from “Update ready” to “Current,” so restart and PWA update happened together and the clips do not prove auto-tracking recovered or isolate the cause. A banner for three pending facility edits remained; the owner confirmed they made no facility-setting edits during the recordings. Later on 2026-11-09, the owner reported that the app was behaving normally while between runs, after observing auto-tracking fail to recover properly. A 32.97-second recording captured at about 11:38 a.m. showed a selected run marked Not Started, Pack/Dough/Run tab navigation, a finished-case surplus form entering its Saving state, and the prior run's freezer drain view; it did not show an active run or an auto-track claim, and the visible Saving state alone does not establish server confirmation. A follow-up 40-second recording around 11:40–11:41 a.m. showed an active run with no visible recovery banner; line-assembly display progressed from 3 to 5 and the previous run's freezer-drain count from 49 to 50, while the selected run's Packaging counters remained at 0/3 skids and 0/54 cases in sampled frames. The clip does not show a completed current-run Packaging claim or server receipt, and the sampled countdown does not prove continuous countdown behavior. Treat this as evidence of partial active-run progress, not proof that all auto-tracking channels recovered. Treat active-run status, stale pending edits, and app-update state as separate comparison signals, not proof of cause.

Another owner-provided recording on 2026-11-09 (about 12:18–12:20 p.m. local device time) began with an active run already showing “Still recovering: checking the current production state” and “Offline — local projection.” After an app-opening splash, the run remained active, but a warning said a correction could not be saved because the section changed and that the edits were still present; facility edits were also shown as awaiting server confirmation. The Packaging view showed “Syncing,” a next-case timer at 0:00 in sampled frames, and no visible progress from the sampled 1/3 skid and 11/54 case values; line assembly remained at 52. The recovery warning and sync/correction warnings persisted through the remainder of the clip, including after navigation through Android app settings/home and return to the app. This is direct client-side evidence of a section-change save conflict and a stalled-looking active Packaging view, but it does not prove that server timers caused the conflict, that a claim was rejected, or that Android app data was cleared.

The owner clarified that only their device was using the app during this incident and that they cleared the app's data; do not attribute this incident to a second-device write. The remaining uncertainty is whether the Android action cleared Chrome's storage for the installed PWA origin or only app-scoped data, and whether any visible warning was reloaded from the server or re-created by a later request.

A follow-up recording around 1:05–1:06 p.m. shows Android Storage for Chrome with 157 MB of app data and 1.26 GB of cache; after “Clear cache,” the cache reads 0 while Chrome app data remains 157 MB. The installed Run Calc app then shows its small app-scoped data/cache cleared, but after reopening it the facility-edit queue and rejected-correction notice remain; the page later shows “Confirmed server baseline.” This indicates Chrome origin/site data was not cleared in that sequence, so local-storage records could remain. Do not ask the owner to clear more data while unsent facility edits are still reported; the recording proves a successful baseline read, not Packaging auto-track recovery.

The owner clarified that no edits have been made since the first run, that the app has acted up since then, and that it is only intermittently normal between runs. Do not attribute the current symptom to a fresh manual edit; the visible rejected-correction notice may be a retained earlier rejection. Prioritize the active-run versus between-run split and separate client wake recovery from server-owned automatic claims.

Another 2026-11-06 screenshot showed “App updates · Update failed” while the Run page still showed a provisional baseline and Start Run. The owner clarified that the app updated successfully at the last publish and no newer release has been published since. A separate public app-build metadata probe succeeded, but that does not prove the handset fetched it. Treat PWA build/update confirmation and operational sync confirmation as separate signals; neither alone proves the other failed, and do not describe this as a failed install of a new release.

**Why:** This makes background work, pool acquisition, timers, readiness during sparse traffic, wake-time client recovery, volatile client state, and cross-client interaction important correlation targets. A cold reopen clearing symptoms may reset client state but can also coincide with network or server recovery. Browser wait can include time suspended in the background; aggregate probes still cannot establish user-request impact or causality.

**How to apply:** In production investigations, compare warm return with cold reopen and correlate both with background-worker, database-pool, request, and client responsiveness telemetry. Include a two-client case where one stays foreground while another wakes. Separately measure tab navigation, in-screen control actions, timer progress, and auto-track claim/commit after a manual edit and Resume. For a pending lifecycle tap, record whether the canonical recovery pull completed, whether the tap was applied or retained, and whether refresh discarded it; verify server lifecycle state rather than inferring it from the restored UI. Keep user observation separate from independently measured evidence; distinguish client elapsed wait from server processing, and do not infer the initiating cause from timing alone.

## Approval to repair after investigation

The owner clarified that choosing “investigate further” means to continue causal investigation, not to decline a fix. This applies consistently to prior and future investigations. Evidence-supported changes are approved once the relevant investigation is exhausted; do not ask for another approval solely because a repair is needed.

**Why:** The owner has chosen further investigation several times and clarified that these choices defer repair until the evidence is sufficient; they do not refuse the repair.

**How to apply:** Complete the relevant client, server, and background-work evidence pass, then make the narrow supported repairs and verify them. Ask again only if new product behavior or policy choices are needed.

## Approved no-work auto-track boundary

The owner approved a conservative server auto-track optimization: skip a date transaction only when the existing claim builders show no due claims and wall-clock bookkeeping is unchanged. Missing, malformed, or uncertain state must use the existing locked transaction. Keep tick cadence, claim authority, outbox polling, other worker cadence, and pool sizing unchanged.

**Why:** The owner suspects idle background work contributes to pool pressure, but preserving server authority and restart-safe claim bookkeeping takes priority over reducing work.

**How to apply:** Use this boundary when reducing scheduled auto-track database work. Validate both the idle skip and active/uncertain fallbacks; do not infer production impact without a measured before/after window.
